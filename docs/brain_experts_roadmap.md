# Archimedes Divided-Brain — Expert Roadmap

The brain is not one model. It is many small domain-expert models, each
trained from scratch on a single domain, composed by an orchestrator that
routes questions to experts, encodes queries into each expert's format, and
decodes/combines their outputs into one answer.

Experts are grouped by how well they fit our proven method — a synthetic
reasoning curriculum where every training answer is generated by a rule and
independently verified (how the math lobe reached 90%). Domains that are
procedural and checkable are our strength; knowledge-heavy domains need
corpora and are harder to synthesize.

Standard lobe size: 117M params (the `small` config), so experts stay
swappable and the divided-brain-vs-monolith comparison is clean.

---

## Tier A — Procedural / rule-based (our sweet spot)

Deterministic, checkable answers. We can generate unlimited verified training
traces, exactly as we did for arithmetic. Build these first.

1. **Mathematics** — ✅ built (v9, 90%). Expanding: arithmetic, algebra,
   calculus (done in curriculum), then trig, geometry, linear algebra,
   differential equations, number theory, combinatorics.
2. **Physics** — 🔨 in progress (corpus building). Mechanics, energy,
   thermodynamics, waves/optics, electromagnetism, modern physics. Formula
   selection → substitution → arithmetic (reuses the math lobe's traces).
3. **Chemistry** — stoichiometry, balancing equations, molar mass, gas laws,
   thermochemistry, solution concentration. Highly procedural; very learnable.
   Directly relevant to the "understand/compose elements" goal.
4. **Computer Science / Code** — algorithms, data structures, complexity,
   code tracing. Uniquely verifiable: answers can be checked by execution.
5. **Formal Logic & Proof** — propositional/predicate logic, proof techniques
   (induction, contradiction). We already have proof templates in the math
   curriculum; this generalizes them.
6. **Statistics & Probability** — descriptive stats, distributions,
   combinatorics, hypothesis tests, Bayesian updates. Procedural and checkable.
7. **Units & Dimensional Analysis** — a small utility expert: unit conversion,
   dimensional consistency, SI prefixes. Supports every science lobe.

## Tier B — Science knowledge + reasoning (corpus-heavy, harder)

Need real corpora; reasoning traces are partly synthesizable but many answers
are factual rather than derived. Build after Tier A proves the composition.

8. **Biology** — molecular, cellular, genetics, physiology. Knowledge-heavy.
9. **Earth & Climate Science** — geology, atmosphere, oceanography, climate.
10. **Astronomy & Cosmology** — orbital mechanics (procedural), plus
    astrophysical knowledge.
11. **Materials Science** — crystal structures, properties, phase diagrams.
    The lobe closest to the "design/discover materials" ambition.
12. **Engineering** — mechanical, electrical, civil. Applied physics + math;
    strong procedural core.

## Tier C — Connective tissue (the glue that makes it a brain)

Not domain experts — the integration layer. These are what turn a pile of
experts into a mind, and where the project's real novelty lives.

13. **Language & Reasoning cortex** — parses natural-language questions,
    decomposes multi-domain problems, composes expert outputs into fluent
    answers. Tightly coupled to the orchestrator.
14. **General Knowledge** — encyclopedic factual grounding; the fallback when
    no specialist owns a query.
15. **The Orchestrator** — routing (which expert[s]?), encoding (query →
    each expert's trained format), decoding/composition (combine outputs,
    feed one expert's result into another). This is the crux research problem
    and the thing big labs are not solving for small-expert systems.

---

## Sequencing rationale

- **Math → Physics → Chemistry** first: each builds on the previous
  (physics uses math; chemistry uses both), and all three are procedural
  enough for verified curricula. Three Tier-A lobes prove breadth.
- **Orchestrator becomes real at two lobes.** The moment physics trains, the
  router/encoder/decoder stops being a diagram and becomes buildable — build
  a first version then, don't wait for all experts.
- **Tier B waits** until the orchestrator works, because knowledge-heavy
  lobes are more expensive and less certain; prove the composition machinery
  on the cheap procedural lobes first.
- **Scale (bigger experts) tracks funding.** 117M is proof-of-concept size;
  deeper competence per lobe needs more compute — the grant ask.

## Honest scope note

This roadmap builds toward broad, reliable, composable scientific competence —
a system that solves and checks real math/physics/chemistry problems across
domains. It does not, by itself, reach autonomous scientific discovery
("invent new elements"): that is a capability frontier beyond current AI, not
a matter of adding more lobes. The realistic and still-valuable target is an
AI that accelerates calculation, derivation, and hypothesis exploration.
